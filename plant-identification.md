### 1. Image 1: Jelly Bean Plant
*   **English Common Name:** Jelly Bean Plant, Pork and Beans
*   **Latin/Scientific Name:** *Sedum rubrotinctum* (often sold as the hybrid *Sedum × rubrotinctum*)
*   **Appearance:** Characterized by plump, bean-shaped leaves that are green in the center and turn a vibrant red or pink at the tips when exposed to sufficient sunlight.

**Care Instructions:**
*   **Light:** Needs bright, direct light (at least 6 hours a day) to maintain its compact shape and red coloration. In low light, it will stretch out (etiolate) and turn entirely green.
*   **Watering:** Water thoroughly only when the soil is completely dry. These plants are drought-tolerant and prone to rot if kept wet. Reduce watering significantly in winter.
*   **Temperature:** Prefers temperatures between 65°F–75°F (18°C–24°C). It can tolerate brief dips down to 30°F (-1°C) but should be brought indoors if freezing is expected.
*   **Fertilizing:** Feed with a balanced, water-succulent fertilizer diluted to half strength once during the spring growing season. Do not fertilize in winter.

**3 Care Tips:**
1.  **Sun Stress is Good:** Don’t be afraid of strong sun; "stressing" the plant with high light and slight underwatering is what creates the beautiful red tips.
2.  **Easy Propagation:** The leaves detach very easily. If one falls off, leave it on the soil surface, and it will likely root and grow into a new plant.
3.  **Watch for Pests:** They are susceptible to mealybugs (white cottony fluff) in the crevices where leaves meet the stem. Treat with neem oil or alcohol swabs if seen.

### 2. Image 2: Echeveria (Likely 'Blue Curls' or similar)
*   **English Common Name:** Hens and Chicks (general term), Blue Echeveria
*   **Latin/Scientific Name:** *Echeveria* species (Likely *Echeveria 'Blue Curls'* or *Echeveria derenbergii* hybrid)
*   **Appearance:** A rosette-forming succulent with thick, fleshy, blue-green leaves covered in a powdery coating called "farina."

**Care Instructions:**
*   **Light:** Requires full sun to partial shade. At least 4–6 hours of direct sunlight is needed to keep the rosette tight. Insufficient light causes the plant to flatten and stretch.
*   **Watering:** Use the "soak and dry" method. Water deeply until it runs out the drainage holes, then wait until the soil is 100% dry before watering again. Avoid getting water on the leaves to prevent rot.
*   **Temperature:** Ideal range is 65°F–80°F (18°C–27°C). Most Echeverias are not frost-hardy and must be protected from temperatures below 30°F (-1°C).
*   **Fertilizing:** Apply a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer in the spring. Over-fertilizing can cause weak, leggy growth.

**3 Care Tips:**
1.  **Protect the Farina:** The white powdery coating on the leaves protects the plant from sunburn and pests. Handle the plant by the base/stem, not the leaves, to avoid wiping this powder off.
2.  **Drainage is Critical:** These plants hate "wet feet." Ensure your pot has large drainage holes and use a gritty, fast-draining soil mix (cactus/succulent mix with added perlite).
3.  **Remove Dead Leaves:** As the plant grows, lower leaves may shrivel and die. Gently remove them to prevent pests from hiding in the decaying matter.

### 3. Image 3: Crassula 'Buddha's Temple'
*   **English Common Name:** Buddha's Temple, Pagoda Plant
*   **Latin/Scientific Name:** *Crassula perforata* var. *brevifolia* (or the hybrid *Crassula* 'Buddha's Temple')
*   **Appearance:** Distinctive stacked, geometric leaves that form a square column, resembling a pagoda or temple structure.

**Care Instructions:**
*   **Light:** Bright, indirect light to partial direct sun. While they need light to stay compact, intense afternoon sun can scorch the leaves. East-facing windows are ideal.
*   **Watering:** Allow the top inch of soil to dry out between waterings. Because the leaves are stacked tightly, water can get trapped inside the stem, leading to rot. Water carefully around the base.
*   **Temperature:** Prefers average room temperatures between 60°F–75°F (15°C–24°C). Protect from drafts and freezing temperatures.
*   **Fertilizing:** Feed monthly during the active growing season (spring/summer) with a balanced liquid fertilizer diluted to half strength.

**3 Care Tips:**
1.  **Avoid Top-Watering:** Never pour water directly onto the top of the "stack." This causes the inner leaves to rot quickly. Always water the soil directly.
2.  **Top-Heavy Stability:** As these grow tall, they become top-heavy and can tip over. Use a heavy ceramic pot or stake the plant if it gets too tall.
3.  **Propagation by Cuttings:** Unlike the Jelly Bean plant, this one is best propagated by stem cuttings. Cut a healthy section of the stack, let it callous over for a few days, and then plant it in dry soil.
